A Study on Improving Thresher Efficiency
      Using Deep Neural Networks


          A computer-science thesis
                                         Abstract


Threshing efficiency and mechanical stability are usually set by a fixed command or by
a moisture card. Both ignore the axial load shape, and both are blind to an interaction
between a heavy feed and a wet crop. This thesis treats the thresher as a plant and
studies two computational problems on it. The first is to identify a discrete operating
status from a short sensor window, after the axial stations have been placed by a design
criterion rather than by habit. The second is to map the resulting features to four
normalized setpoints—intensity, residence, cleaning, and feed admission—with a deep
network trained to imitate a numerical optimizer.

  The status model is a feedforward network on window statistics. On a held-out mixture
of operating conditions it reaches 89.8% accuracy and a macro-F1 of 0.846, against 82.6%
and 0.734 for a linear softmax on the same features. The gain is concentrated on the
spatial and the rare labels: front-loaded recall rises from 0.56 to 0.79, and under-filled
recall from 0.27 to 0.61. A D-optimal design for a quadratic load shape puts stations at
the inlet, at two central bands, and at the discharge. Accuracy saturates once the two
ends are instrumented, which is what a contrast feature needs; the four-station design is
kept because the control features also use the curvature of the load.

  The control network is trained by squared error against the optimizer, on features
drawn under a fixed command, under the moisture card, and under random commands.
On a weighted seasonal mix of wheat, rice, and soybean it lowers grain loss from 2.85% at
the fixed setting and 2.50% on the moisture card to 1.80%, against 1.78% for the optimizer
itself. Specific shaft energy rises from 1.41 to 1.63 kWh t−1 . A stability index whose design
floor is 0.70 rises from 0.686 to 0.701. Impurity rises from 1.17% to 1.50% and a breakage
index from 1.04% to 1.49%. Those two rises are the prices inside the optimizer’s objective,
not side effects discovered after the fact. The numbers are a numerical experiment on a
reduced-order plant. They are not a field trial.

Chapter 1

Background and Problem Statement

The machine in this thesis is a thresher. The contribution is not a new machine. It is a
pair of computations that a fixed setting and a moisture card do not perform: a placement
of sensors and a feature model that identify the operating status, and a deep network
that turns those features into commands which raise grain recovery while a stability floor,
an energy price, an impurity price, and a breakage price remain in force. This chapter
says what the plant is, what the published learning systems already do with machines of
this kind, and which two problems the later chapters are required to answer.



1.1     Structure, Operating Principles, and Types of

        Threshers

1.1.1    What the computer is allowed to mean by the machine

A thresher frees grain from ears or pods and delivers the grain without the straw. Oper-
ators learn a setting on a uniform crop and leave it there while the feed and the moisture
change. A moisture card is the same habit written down: wetter grain is given a harder
or a longer pass, and the card does not read the load. The computer-science object is
the map from a window of measurements to the next command. The metal exists in this
thesis only so that the map has inputs, outputs, and a cost.

  The topology used from here on has four blocks. A feeder admits biomass. A threshing

                                            5
unit applies impact and rubbing against a concave, and grain that is free can leave through
that concave. A cleaner, made of air and oscillating sieves, then accepts grain and rejects
chaff. Material that the cleaner does not decide may be offered to the inlet again. Straw
leaves at the rear. Figure 1.1 is that topology drawn as a plant, with the quantities the
later chapters actually name.

1.1.2     Operating principle, stated as quantities

Three failures define a bad pass, and they are different failures. Grain that is still attached
when the straw leaves is an unthreshed loss. Free grain that is still in the straw is a
separation loss. Grain that the cleaner throws away is a sieve loss. Chaff that stays in
the sample is impurity, which is not a loss of grain and must not be added to one. A
faster or a longer pass can cut the first two losses and can crack kernels. A more open
cleaner can cut the third loss and can raise impurity. Power rises with intensity and with
residence. None of these signs is controversial; they are the reason a single knob is not a
controller [28, 24, 25].

  Mechanical stability, as the word is used here, is not a stress analysis of a shaft. It is
an index of the running machine. The axial load should not pile up at the inlet while
the discharge runs light. The peak load should stay clear of a blockage level. Vibration
should stay in proportion to the intensity that was asked for. Chapter 2 writes the index
S ∈ [0, 1] from those three signals and puts a floor under it. A command that buys
recovery by crossing the floor is a failed command even if the loss number looks better.

  The feed and the moisture do not act as separate additives. A heavy feed on a dry

                                                6
crop and a heavy feed on a wet crop are different plants: the wet, heavy case packs the
inlet and raises unthreshed loss together, which is a product, not a sum. A card that is
linear in moisture cannot represent a product. That algebraic fact is the reason a linear
command map is kept in the study as an ablation rather than assumed to be enough.



1.1.3     Types, and why the axial plant is the one that is simulated

Published descriptions group threshers by the path of the crop [26, 27, 28].

  A tangential machine presents the crop to a drum for a fraction of one revolution. The
concave is short. Separation after the drum is done by a straw walker or by a rotary
separator. There is little axial shape to measure on the drum itself, and a single load on
the concave is a fair summary of that station. The control problem then collapses toward
a choice of drum speed and a single gap.

  An axial machine keeps the crop in a cylindrical cage for many revolutions. Helical
guides set how fast the mat walks from inlet to discharge. The load then has a shape.
The inlet carries the full straw and the still-attached grain. The discharge carries straw
and whatever free grain has not yet left. A uniform transport speed makes a front-heavy
shape, because grain is leaving along the axis while the straw is not. One load cell on the
whole concave sees the sum and misses the shape. A row of stations sees the shape. That
row is a placement problem: which stations, and how many, are worth the channels.

  A hold-on or stationary thresher changes the feeder and not the maps. The inlet may
be a belt or a person rather than a header. Unthreshed loss, separation loss, sieve loss,
impurity, and power still move with moisture and with how hard and how long the crop
is worked. The sensor question is unchanged whenever the threshing unit is axial.

  The numerical plant in Chapters 2 and 3 is axial, with eight possible load stations
along the cage. The tangential machine is the comparison that explains the placement
problem. It is not simulated. The stationary feeder is the reason feed admission is a
command at all: the computer may trim the inlet for a short time without claiming to
drive a vehicle.




                                            7
1.1.4    The interface the rest of the thesis is allowed to use

The disturbance is the crop, the offered biomass feed q, and the moisture m. Three crops
appear: wheat, rice, and soybean. They differ by grain fraction, by a difficulty multiplier
on the loss and breakage maps, and by a multiplier on the load. They do not differ by a
change of network architecture.

  The command is four numbers in the unit interval. Intensity uI stands for how hard the
threshing unit hits. Residence uR stands for how long the mat is kept over the concave.
Cleaning uC stands for how aggressively the air-and-sieve stage sorts. Feed admission
uF trims the inlet over a limited range; it is not a license to shut the machine and call
the idle state efficient. The mechanism that turns these four numbers into a rotor speed,
a transport setting, a sieve, and a feeder speed is outside the learning method. The
simulator in Chapter 2 is the only place they have a numerical effect.

  The measurement window contains the instrumented station loads, a shaft torque,
a housing vibration, a sieve-loss proxy, a return flow, shaft power, moisture, and feed.
Torque and power see the level of the load. They do not see which station is hot.
Vibration is taken at the housing and is modeled as a function of intensity, moisture, and
throughput, not of the axial peak. The peak’s location is left to the stations. That split
is deliberate. If a scalar sensor already contained the shape, a paper about placement
would be arguing with its own sensor model.

  Grain-recovery efficiency is recovered grain divided by grain offered. Cutting the feed
lowers it, so a policy cannot improve the headline number by refusing crop. Grain loss
L is the fraction of admitted grain that is unthreshed, free at the rear, or thrown by the
cleaner. When the feed trim is fully open the two statements differ only by rounding.
Specific energy e is shaft energy per tonne of admitted grain. Chapter 3 also reports the
shaft power that this specific energy implies, so the two cannot drift apart unnoticed.

1.2      Artificial Intelligence Applied to Threshers

1.2.1     What the surveys already separate

Two surveys of learning in agriculture, and one survey of combine control, are enough
to locate the gap. Kamilaris and Prenafeta-Boldú group deep models in the field by the
task the network is asked to perform, and the mass of the work is perception: weeds,
fruit, disease, and land cover, with convolutional networks on images [16]. Liakos and
co-authors make the same cut from the other direction. Machine learning in agriculture
is mostly yield, disease, and species decisions, and the machinery papers are a thin slice
beside them [17]. Chen, Qian, Jin, and Yang review control algorithms on grain combines
and find the opposite emphasis: rotor speed, sieve opening, fan speed, and ground speed,
closed one loop at a time, with learning used where a model of the cleaner or the header
is inconvenient [19]. Perception papers and actuator papers cite each other and solve
different problems.

   A third survey, on vision for grain crops, makes the perception side concrete. Counting
kernels, locating unthreshed heads, and scoring impurity from an image are detection
problems, and the published systems treat the detector’s output as the product [18]. A
detector can name a loss. It does not by itself choose a command vector under a stability
floor.



1.2.2     Loss sensing and single-loop control

Impact plates under a sieve made grain loss a real-time count rather than a bag weighed
at the end of a row. Liang, Li, Xu, and Zhao built that sensor for rice and studied where
along the sieve a plate has to sit if the count is to stand for the shoe [22]. Zhao, Li, Chen,
and Xu had already closed a monitoring loop of the same family [23]. These papers are
about observability of one term. They are not about the axial shape, and they are not
about a joint command.

   The cleaning controller that still structures the actuator literature is the fuzzy system
of Craessaerts, De Baerdemaeker, Missotten, and Saeys [20]. It trades sieve loss, return


                                              9
volume, and impurity, and it moves a fan and a sieve opening. Return volume is a penalty
in the cost. It is not a delayed state. Shoe frequency, stroke, and a gate are outside the
command. The identification underneath the controller was done with the rotor held in
one condition, so the axial load never enters the features.

  Model-predictive cruise control on the combine, as in the Leuven line, moves ground
speed from a process model and a horizon [21, 15]. It is the right computation when the
command is a speed and the constraint is a capacity. It is a different command from
the four setpoints in this thesis, and it assumes a model the designer already trusts at
the horizon. The optimizer in Chapter 2 is in that computational family: a numerical
minimization of a scalar cost subject to bounds [11]. It is used as a teacher, offline, with
the disturbance known. It is not proposed as the program that has to run from a noisy
window every control period.



1.2.3     What is not in those papers

The published pieces can be lined up without caricature.

    A vision or impact model estimates a loss or an impurity. The estimate is the
     output.

    A fuzzy or predictive loop moves one or two actuators. The axial load shape is not
     a feature, and a stability floor on that shape is not a constraint.

    A threshing balance of the Miu–Kutzbach kind predicts loss along a machine once
     the rates are known [24]. It is a plant model. It does not place sensors, and it does
     not approximate its own optimizer with a network.

  Two computational objects are missing from that list. The first is a placement. Eight
possible stations and a budget K are a design of experiments on a shape model [10],
followed by a check of whether the status labels that the shape induces are actually
recovered by a classifier. The second is a function from the resulting features to the
teacher’s command. The teacher can afford a numerical solve with the true disturbance
in hand. The onboard program cannot. A feedforward network is a standard approximant


                                            10
of a continuous map on a compact set [4, 5, 1]. Whether this particular map needs the
depth, or whether a linear map on the same features already matches it, is an empirical
question the study is required to answer rather than to assume. The status boundary is
a different question. Front-loaded, blockage-risk, and high-loss share some of the same
sensors and are separated by thresholds on different quantities. A linear softmax and a
small ReLU network do not have to tie on that boundary [8, 9].

  Recurrent networks are the right tool when the raw series is the input and the relevant
time is long [2]. Here the window is short and is reduced to statistics before the network
sees it. The architecture that matches that choice is feedforward. Chapter 2 does not
train a recurrent net and then claim a comparison it did not run.



1.3        Problems Addressed by This Thesis

1.3.1      Problem 1: status from a designed placement

Let C be a label in


             {stable, front-loaded, under-filled, blockage-risk, high-loss},


assigned from the full load shape, the loss, and the mean load by the priority rule in
Chapter 2. The rule uses the true stations. The classifier does not. It sees a window
from a subset p of the eight stations, together with the scalar channels that are always
present.

  Problem 1. Choose a placement p with |p| = K, a feature map x, and a classifier
Ĉ(x), so that Ĉ recovers C on conditions that were not used to choose p. Report accuracy
and macro-F1 against a linear softmax on the same features, and report the same figures
for a placement that simply takes the first K stations from the inlet. A design that
raises accuracy by memorizing the training windows is not a solution. The test mixture
is drawn again, with new noise.

  The label set is part of the problem. Accuracy alone is a weak score because a stable
machine is the common case. Macro-F1 gives the rare labels a vote. Under-filled is the

                                           11
thinnest class in the test mixture, and its recall is reported with that fact attached.



1.3.2     Problem 2: commands that raise recovery under a priced
          stability floor

Let u⋆ (q, m, crop) be a maximizer of the objective J defined in Chapter 2. The maximizer
may use the true disturbance. The network may not. It receives x built from a noisy
window that was generated under some other command, and it emits û(x).

  Problem 2. Train û so that the closed-loop plant, under û, improves grain loss
relative to a fixed command and relative to a moisture card, keeps the seasonal mean
of S at the floor 0.70 rather than under it, and stays close to the teacher’s loss, energy,
impurity, and breakage. A solution that cuts loss by refusing feed, or by idling the rotor,
fails the definition of η and fails J. A solution that matches the teacher on average and
misses the wet, heavy row by a wide margin must say so in the point table. The linear
map on the same features is the ablation that decides whether depth is doing work on
this command or only on the status label.



1.3.3     Requirements the later chapters are checked against

 R1. The placement is chosen by a stated criterion on a shape model, and the installed
     set is written down as station numbers.

 R2. The status model and the command model are trained on one sample and scored
     on another. The seasonal points used in Chapter 3 are not the training set.

 R3. The command model does not receive C as an input. Status and command are
     separate networks that share a feature definition. A hidden leak of the true label
     into û would make Problem 2 a different problem.

 R4. Feed admission may move only inside the trim encoded by the plant. Recovery is
     computed on grain offered, so a closed trim is a loss of recovery.

 R5. Energy, impurity, and breakage are reported beside loss. A table that shows only
     loss is not an answer to Problem 2.

                                            12
R6. The onboard program specified in Chapter 3 is a fixed cyclic schedule: build the
    window, standardize, evaluate the two networks, and rate-limit the write. It does
    not call the teacher.

R7. Claims are limited to the reduced-order plant. A field percentage is not among the
    results.



1.3.4   Assumptions that bound the claims

A1. The plant maps in Chapter 2 are smooth, static, and first-order in the losses. A
    rate that saturated harder at high load would move the wet-rice numbers. It would
    not remove the product between feed and moisture that the linear card cannot see.

A2. Station noise, torque noise, and the loss-proxy noise are independent and zero-mean
    at the variances written with the feature map. A shared electrical fault would break
    the median-free design used here; the program’s response to a missing station is
    specified separately and is not claimed to cover a stuck-at bias that looks like a real
    load.

A3. The teacher is a local bound-constrained solve started from a coarse grid [11]. A
    better global search could move a point. The network is trained to imitate the
    teacher that was actually run.

A4. The moisture card is a competent linear policy, not a straw man. It is allowed to
    beat the fixed setting on wet crops. It is not given the feed or the load shape.

A5. No grain price and no fuel price enter J. The weights are design choices, published
    so the tables can be read against the cost that produced them. Changing the
    breakage weight would move residence. The comparison among fixed, card, linear
    map, network, and teacher is at one published weight vector.

A6. The network is a static map of one window. It has no internal state beyond the
    rate limit applied at the write. A slower drift of moisture than the window is
    handled because moisture is itself a feature, not because the network remembers
    the morning.

Application of Deep Neural

The problems in Chapter 1 ask for a placement, a feature map, a status model, and a
command model. This chapter defines them and scores them offline. The plant comes
first, because every label and every teacher command is a function of it. The plant is a
reduced-order simulator. It is not a derivation from a new experiment, and it is not a
sizing of hardware. Its only duty is to land a fixed command in the loss and specific-energy
band already published for axial machines, and to contain the feed–moisture product that
a linear card cannot represent [24, 25].



2.1      Positioning Sensors to Identify Operating Status

2.1.1     Indicators, and the ones that were refused

Operating status is a label of the running machine, not a name for the crop. Five labels
are used, and they are assigned from the true plant output in this priority, so the first
match wins:

  1. blockage-risk, if the peak station load is at least 0.84 × 1680 N;

                                            15
   2. high-loss, if grain loss is at least 2.8%;

   3. front-loaded, if the inlet station exceeds the discharge station by at least 0.38 of
      the mean load and the peak is at least 760 N;

   4. under-filled, if the mean station load is at most 430 N;

   5. stable, otherwise.

  The priority is the model of “what an operator would say first.” A machine that is
both front-loaded and past the blockage line is blockage-risk. A machine that is merely
light is under-filled only if no sharper failure is present.

  The indicators that can see these labels are not a catalog of every transducer on a
combine. They are the smallest set whose absences are informative.

    Axial station loads see the level, the peak, and the contrast. Without at least two
      stations that are far apart, front-loaded is not a measurement.

    Shaft torque and shaft power see the level a second time. They do not see which
      station produced it. They stay in the feature vector as scalars that are always
      installed.

    Housing vibration sees intensity, moisture, and throughput. In the simulator it
      does not depend on the peak station. Putting the peak into the vibration channel
      would have made the placement study circular.

    A sieve-loss proxy sees the cleaner term that high-loss partly reflects. It does not
      see the axial shape.

    Return flow is kept because it moves with sieve loss and is cheap. It is not a separate
      label.

    Moisture and offered feed are measured directly. They are disturbances, and they
      are also legitimate features, because the onboard program is allowed to read the
      sensors the machine already has for them.

  A camera is not in the set. Problem 1 is about whether a short physical window,
after a placement decision, is enough for the five labels. Adding an image channel would

                                              16
answer a different question, the one the perception surveys have already framed [16, 18].

2.1.3     The feature map

A window is eight samples. At the 20 Hz rate used by the program in Chapter 3, the
window is 0.4 s. For each installed station the features are the window mean and the
window standard deviation. From the vector of means the map adds the coefficient of
variation, the contrast between the first and the last installed station divided by the mean,
and the maximum mean. Torque, vibration, the loss proxy, return flow, and power each
contribute a mean and a standard deviation. Moisture and feed contribute one noisy
reading each. Loads are divided by 1000, load standard deviations by 100, the loss proxy
is multiplied by 100, power’s mean is divided by 10, and feed is divided by 10, so that a
standardized network does not have to spend its first epochs on units. With four stations
the raw feature dimension is 23.

  The noise used when the windows are drawn is part of the method, not a hidden
robustness trick. Station loads are perturbed by independent Gaussian noise of standard
deviation 22 N. Torque, vibration, loss proxy, return, and power use standard deviations
0.015, 0.03, 0.0015, 0.012, and 0.15 in the units of those channels. Moisture noise is 0.004
and the scaled-feed noise is 0.012. A stuck channel is not this noise. It is the fault case
in Chapter 3.

  Standardization is affine and is fit on the training windows only:


                                        x̃ = (x − µtr ) ⊘ σ tr ,                           (2.3)


with any coordinate whose training standard deviation is below 10−6 left unscaled. The

                                                    18
same µtr and σ tr are applied to validation, test, and the closed-loop program. Refitting
them on the test mixture would leak the evaluation into the preprocessor.



2.1.4    The status network

The status model is a feedforward net with sizes 23–48–32–5. Hidden layers are ReLU.
The output is a softmax. Initialization is Gaussian with variance 2/nin on each layer,
the scale that keeps ReLU layers from starting saturated [6]. Training minimizes the
multinomial cross-entropy with Adam at learning rate 8 × 10−4 , batches of 64, for 100
epochs [7, 3]. The epoch with the best validation accuracy is the one that is kept. On
the run reported here that epoch is 99. The network has 2885 parameters.

  The linear baseline is a softmax with the same standardized input, no hidden layer,
trained by minibatch gradient descent with ℓ2 weight 10−3 . It is the correct baseline. A
gain of the deep model over a different feature set would not answer Problem 1.

  Labels for training are computed from the noise-free plant at the command that pro-
duced the window. The command is not always the teacher’s command. Each disturbance
is observed three times: under the fixed setting, under the moisture card, and under a
command drawn uniformly from [0.15, 0.95]4 . A classifier trained only at the fixed setting
would not be entitled to a score on the other two, and the program in Chapter 3 has to
recognize status while it is in the middle of changing the command.

2.2.3     The command network

The command model is a second feedforward net, sizes 23–64–64–32–4, ReLU hidden
units, linear output, clipped to [0, 1] when it is applied. It has 7908 parameters and
7744 multiply-adds. The same He-style initialization and the same Adam settings are
used, with learning rate 10−3 for 140 epochs [6, 7]. The kept epoch is the one with the
lowest validation mean squared error, epoch 122 in the reported run, where that error is
1.03 × 10−3 .

  The target is u⋆ . The input is not the feature window at u⋆ . Each training disturbance
contributes three windows, generated under the moisture card, under a random command,
and under the fixed command, all labeled with the same u⋆ . A network trained only at


                                             22
the teacher’s own operating point would be asked, at deployment, to act from the card’s
operating point, which is a different distribution of features. One mixture of the three
conditions is the whole of the correction used here. It is in the spirit of dataset aggregation
and it is not an iterated online reduction [13]. Calling it a no-regret imitation loop would
overstate a single static sample.

   The linear ablation is least squares from the standardized features, plus a bias, onto
u⋆ , with the prediction clipped to [0, 1]. Same inputs, same targets, no hidden layer. If
this map matches the network on the plant, depth is not what moves the grain, and the
thesis has to say that.

   The loss that is trained is squared error on the four commands. It is not J, and it
is not a reinforcement signal. The teacher has already folded J into u⋆ . Training on J
directly would be a different algorithm and would need the plant inside the training loop.
Imitation is the algorithm that matches requirement R6: the onboard program evaluates
a function and does not call the teacher.



2.2.4     What the command network is not

It does not take the status label as an input. A correct label would be a strong feature
and would glue Problem 1 to Problem 2 so tightly that a failure of the classifier would
be invisible in the loss table. The two networks share the feature definition and nothing
else.

   It does not output a rotor speed in revolutions per minute. The four components stay
normalized. Chapter 3 rate-limits each component by 0.08 per 0.2 s at the write. That
limit is a property of the program, not of the loss surface. The offline scores in the next
section are unclipped by the rate limit. The transient in Chapter 3 is where the limit is
on.

3.1      An Automated Control System

3.1.1        What the program is

The control system is one cyclic executive. It does not contain a second optimizer, a vision
pipeline, or a copy of the teacher. At each control step it standardizes a feature window
with the training moments of (2.3), evaluates the status network and the command
network, and writes a rate-limited command. Figure 3.1 is the data path. The four
blocks are ordinary functions. Their only state is the window, the last command, and a
fault bit.

  The tasks and the periods are fixed in advance. Nothing in the executive is a general-
purpose scheduler, because a late feature build must not be free to delay the write by an
unbounded amount [14].

3.1.3     Faults, and the direction of the fallback

A missing axial station is dropped from the contrast and from the coefficient of variation,
and the command is held for that step. If two or more of the four installed stations are
missing, the write is the moisture card until the channels return. A missing moisture
reading holds the last moisture and raises a fault bit. It does not invent a dry crop,
because the card would then move the wrong way. A status confidence below 0.55 selects
the card for that step. On the test windows this gate fires on 2.5% of rows. The seasonal
runs and the slug in Section 3.2 do not inject missing channels, and they do not take the
gate’s credit. They are the networks with a full window. The gate is specified so that
a low-confidence window has a defined output; it is not secretly responsible for the loss
reduction in the tables.

  The card is the fallback in every one of these cases, rather than the fixed setting or a
zero command. The card is a function of moisture, it is always defined, and Chapter 2
already measured it. Falling back to a command that has not been measured would make
the fault logic a third controller.

breakage of 1.27%. Loss looks better than the teacher and the objective does not, because
breakage and energy were the terms the teacher refused to spend. This is the row where
imitation error is visible as a purchase, not as a random residual. It is also the row that

  On wet rice the fixed setting loses 4.99%, sits at stability 0.54, and peaks at 1441 N,

of variation on this row falls from 0.157 at the fixed setting to 0.047 under the network.
That is the shape term doing the work the card cannot do, and it is why the middle-
and-end placement is in the feature vector even though two stations already classify the
label.

  On light wheat the fixed setting is already above the floor, at 0.76. The teacher spends
the margin down to 0.70, lowers residence to 0.37, and cuts loss from 1.93% to 1.52%. The
network does the same thing: residence 0.38, loss 1.50%, stability 0.70. Extra residence
is not a universal command. The objective’s hinge is the reason. Above 0.70, further
calmness is unpaid, and a light crop should not be held longer for a calmness the cost
does not buy.

  Feed admission stays at 1, or within 0.01 of 1, on every seasonal point and for both the
network and the teacher. The trim is in the command vector because the plant allows it
and because a policy that used it would be visible in the recovery column. Neither the
teacher nor the imitator needs it on this mix. The blockage hinge is met by residence
and intensity instead. A heavier point than wet rice could move the trim. No such point
is in the weighted mean, and none is added after the fact.

3.2.4     What the evaluation does and does not establish

The evaluation establishes four comparisons on this plant, at the published weights of
(2.16), with seed 7.

  The status network on the installed placement is more accurate than a linear softmax
on the features the placement makes, and the margin is in the spatial and the rare classes.
That result belongs to Chapter 2 and is not re-litigated by the loss tables. A season can
look good while the labeler is weak, because the command net never reads the label.

  The command network reproduces the teacher’s seasonal loss to 0.03 points and the
teacher’s energy, impurity, and breakage to a few hundredths. A linear imitator of the
same teacher recovers most of the same gain and leaves 0.08 points more loss on the
seasonal mean. Depth is a small advantage on the command once the features exist. It
is not zero on this mix, and it is not the reason the card loses.

  The moisture card is a lawful baseline. It helps on wet wheat and on wet rice, and
it hurts on dry, heavy wheat. Any summary that reports only the seasonal mean of the
card will call it a modest improvement and will miss the row it gets backwards.

  The stability floor is met as a seasonal mean and as a minimum along the tested
transient. It is not met as a hard constraint on every random test window: Chapter 2
recorded that 30% of those windows still fall just under 0.70 after imitation, against 0.4%
for the teacher. The tenth percentile on that mixture is 0.693. The executive does not
project û back onto {S ≥ 0.70}. Doing so would require the plant inside the step, which
is the teacher again. The gap is the price of a pure function evaluation, and it should be
read as a gap.

  The evaluation does not establish a field loss of 1.80%. The plant was built so that
a fixed command would sit in a published band of axial loss and specific energy, and
so that feed and moisture would interact. A different breakage weight would move the
dry-heavy overshoot and the wet-rice purchase. The same comparison, at these weights,


                                            35
is what the tables are for: a fixed setting and a moisture card, against a network that
imitates a cost in which those purchases are explicit, with the teacher present in the last
column so the imitation can be seen to succeed and, on two rows, to miss.
