           Perspective Lattice Completion for Side-View Counting
                           of Stacked Beer Crates
                                            Computer Science
                                      System Design and Optimization


                                                       Abstract
          Beer crates on a forklift are a regular lattice seen from the side. A mask count of that face misses
     the ranks behind it and also misses shadowed or clipped cells. This paper estimates the count by snapping
     rectified mask centers to the known crate pitch, filling a single empty cell that has two orthogonal neighbors,
     and multiplying by a depth reading. On a Monte Carlo model of detection, the lattice counter has mean
     absolute error 3.01 crates against 4.87 for masks times depth, 7.26 for an area ratio, and 3.31 for a span
     fill of the bounding rectangle. Unioning five frames at a vote of one raises the error to 5.80 with a positive
     bias of 5.33. The recommended counter uses one frame.

Keywords—object counting, instance segmentation, lattice, warehouse inventory.


1    Introduction
The image at a brewery gate shows the front face of a stack. The count the warehouse needs includes the crates
behind that face. If the pallet is three deep, a perfect segmentation of the visible face is wrong by a factor of
three. No gain in mask average precision repairs a factor of three. Depth is a small integer, one to three in the
loads considered here, and it is treated as a reading with a known error rate rather than as something the side
image is asked to invent.
    The front face itself is not a crowd. Returnable crates of one family have a nominal face of 0.40 m by 0.28 m.
They are placed on a palletizer grid. Centers that survive detection fall nearer to their own cell than to a
neighbor, once the face has been rectified. That regularity is the information an area ratio does not use and a
density map cannot store. Perspective lattice completion (PLC) turns it into an integer: occupied cells on the
rectified face, times the depth reading.
    The estimate uses a generator of stacks and of missed detections. It is not a count from a filmed passage.
The comparison is still informative, because every counter sees the same centers and the same depth reading.


2    Related work
Learned counters that integrate a density were built for crowds and for dotted annotations [1, 2]. The integral
does not remember color, and it does not add a hidden rank. Detection counting preserves identity and is
biased low by every miss. An area-ratio inventory divides each visible mask by a nominal full area and sums
the fractions [3]. On tobacco cargo with Mask2Former that ratio is a reported inventory accuracy of 93.90%.
The ratio is the right tool when the goods have no pitch. When the pitch is known, a fraction below one is a
biased estimator of a cell that is either present or absent.
    Counting stacked identical objects from several views can be posed as a volume times an occupancy ratio [4].
That decomposition fits a jumble in a bin. It is a poor fit to an integer depth of identical crates and a single
side view lasting a second or two. Rotated lightweight detectors address yaw and occlusion of logistics boxes [5]
and still return boxes, not a depth-multiplied lattice. A carton dataset shows that repeated texture glues

A homography H, fit once at installation from a planar target [7, 8], sends a mask center to metres (x, y) on
the front plane. The pitch (w, h) = (0.40, 0.28) is known and the origin is not, because the truck does not stop
on a mark. The origin is the median residual of the centers after each has been snapped to a lattice that was

3.3    One pass of completion
Let D be the cells that received a center, and let F start as D. Inside the bounding rectangle, an empty cell
is added if at least two of its four orthogonal neighbors are already in F. The pass is left to right, bottom to
top, and the update is immediate. One neighbor is the outside of a solid stack and must not grow a rind. Two
neighbors is the corner of a hole. The same test fills a step in the true outline: a top row that really contains
only the right-hand crate presents the empty cell beside it with a lower neighbor and a right neighbor.
    Depth is not inferred from the masks. The reading dˆ equals d with probability 0.90 and otherwise moves
by one, clipped to {1, 2, 3}. A wrong reading costs about |F | crates. No gap rule repairs it, so the reading is
shown beside the total.

4     Estimate
Each trial draws two to six rows, two to four columns, and depth one to three. Cells are occupied with
probability 0.93. A blur variable lowers detection probability by up to 0.18. At zero blur the bottom row is
kept with probability 0.70, the top with 0.84, and a middle row with 0.96. A detected center is the cell center
plus the Gaussian noise above. False centers arrive as a Poisson count of mean 0.35. Five frames are drawn.
Four thousand trials are run. The mean truth is 22.62 crates.
    Table 1 separates three effects. Ignoring depth is an almost pure under-count: mean absolute error 13.55
and bias −13.53. Multiplying the same masks by the reading cuts the absolute error to 4.87. That is the largest
single reduction. The area ratio is worse than those masks, because a fraction centered at 0.86 short-changes
a stack that was already slightly short. This does not refute an area-ratio result on irregular cargo [3]. It says
a fraction below one is the wrong summary of a missed crate of known size.
    Given depth, the span reaches absolute error 3.31 and bias +1.60. PLC on one frame reaches 3.01 and +0.83,
and is exact on 0.337 of trials. The absolute gap is three tenths of a crate on a load of about twenty-three.
The bias gap is the signature of the neighbor test: both methods over-fill, and the test over-fills less than a
solid rectangle. The five-frame union is a nearly pure over-count, bias +5.33 against absolute error 5.80. Five
frames are worse than one. By true depth, PLC on one frame stays ahead of the span: absolute errors 1.72,
3.27, and 4.03 at depths one, two, and three, against 1.91, 3.59, and 4.40.
    One drawn stack makes the positive bias countable. The front occupancy is three full rows of three and
a top row with only the right-hand crate, ten cells, depth two, truth 20. The first frame has nine detections.
The rule restores the miss and also fills the middle of the top step, eleven cells, booked total 22. The span fills
both empty top cells and books 24. The five-frame union books 28. The hand rule and the instrument agree
with each other and not with the truth.
    A wrong depth reading contributes on the order of one crate of absolute error when the front has about ten
cells and the error rate is ten percent. That term is shared by every counter that multiplies by d. ˆ Halving the
rate is worth about as much as the entire gap between the span and PLC. The design priority is a depth value
from the pallet record, and vision effort spent on the front face.

5    What is displayed
The booked record shows the total, the completed-cell count |F \ D|, the median origin residual, and whether
dˆ was a default. An exact total with a positive completed-cell count can be a cancellation: one unrestored miss
and one phantom. The exact-match rate of 0.337 is not the fraction of passages that were correct for the right
reason. The bias under one crate says phantoms slightly outweigh remaining misses. A passage is refused when
the buffer is empty or the origin residual exceeds half a cell. A zero count is reserved for an empty pallet.


6    What this estimate does not say
The detection probability already folds shadow, blur, and suppression into one number per row. A real threshold
on non-maximum suppression would have to be tuned against labeled frames before anyone treated Table 1 as
a prediction of that threshold. Merged seams are the other gap. If two crates arrive as one center, they occupy
one cell, and the fill rule does not invent the missing neighbor unless some other detection supports it. The
vertical-feature gate in the baseline segmenter is the intended defense against that merge. This paper does not
estimate the gate.
     The visit order is part of the method that was measured. A right-to-left pass on the stepped stack would
fill the corner as well as the middle cell, and PLC would then agree with the span at 24 on the worked example.
Changing the order is a different counter and needs its own row. The row in the table is left to right, immediate
update, threshold two.


7    Conclusion
Side-view crate counting is a lattice times an integer depth, not a density and not a sum of partial areas.
Snapping centers to the known pitch and filling only a doubly supported hole is a small improvement on filling
the bounding span and a large improvement on counting masks. The improvement does not justify fusing every
frame of the passage at a vote of one: that union invents cells. The counter to run is PLC on the single sharpest
frame, with the depth reading shown beside the total, until a filmed gate says otherwise.
