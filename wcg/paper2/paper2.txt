Multi-Factor Model-Predictive Coordination for a Wooden Seed
Sowing Machine

Abstract
Vacuum, vibration, and depth are usually tuned one laboratory slice at a time, then left as
constants on the machine. Wooden seeds make that practice fail in opposite directions at once:
a vacuum that seats a small acorn doubles it in a large cell, and a vibration that unlocks a
heavy seed fluidises a light one. This short paper specifies a slow coordinator that chooses
shaker frequency and assist vacuum by minimising a logistic cost of miss probability, double
probability, spacing scatter, and actuator effort. Depth is held by a servo at 30 mm. The search
is blind to any vision constraint on feed speed, so it cannot hide a bad regime behind a slower
wheel. On the numerical model of the parent thesis, the coordinator selects zero vacuum in
every reported lot. Under a mild 6 Hz disturbance it selects about 9 Hz for red oak and raises
planting efficiency from 61% with speed tracking alone to 77%. Under a 16 Hz disturbance
the 3 Hz and 9 Hz costs nearly tie, and the recorded run selects 3 Hz, lifting efficiency from
22% to 64%. The figures are model solutions, not a field trial. They rank a constrained search
against a fixed cereal default.

Introduction

A precision meter has more than one input. Shaft speed sets the release rate. Vacuum, vibration,
and opener depth change whether a cell fills and whether the seed stays where it was placed.
Cereal studies map these factors with response surfaces and publish a setpoint [1, 2]. Vibration
studies find a frequency above which a rapeseed meter loses discharge, near 10 Hz in one holewheel rig, while an irregular vegetable seeder is helped by 50 Hz and a substantial suction [3, 4, 5].
Copying either setpoint onto an acorn is a category error. A pneumatic hardwood meter metered
pin oak and red oak and failed on bur oak and black walnut [6]. The cell did not change in the
field.
Model-predictive control was built to re-solve a constrained problem as the measurements move
[7, 8, 9]. This paper uses the smallest version that matches the sensors on a single-row woodenseed planter: one statistical step, an exhaustive grid, and a slew limit. The feed-speed loop is a
different paper. Here the shaft is assumed to track the kinematic command. The contribution is
the factor decision.

At the nominal red-oak diameter, a 6 Hz terrain, and the pessimistic weight, J is 2.17 at 9 Hz

a 16 Hz terrain the costs at 3 Hz and 9 Hz differ only in the fourth digit. Middle frequencies are
worse. The search therefore rejects the middle of the band and rejects a standing vacuum. It does
not, on this cost, identify a unique frequency once the frame is already shaking.

What the coordinator is not allowed to see

The instantaneous bridge index is withheld. If the cost could see it, the search would learn to
leave a bridge in place and let a feed constraint clean it up, which looks good in a joint predictor
and poor in seeds per minute. The tube count of the last second is also withheld. That count is
the outcome. A slow trim of the logistic intercepts, over a minute, would be a reasonable later
adaptation. It is not part of this design. Health bits pass between computers. They change mode.
They do not enter J as if they were diameters.

Speed tracking recovers a slope, which is why the 12◦ PID efficiency matches the level row, and
it does little about vibration or seed size. The search is the large step. Zero vacuum in every row
is the map refusing a cereal default that doubles a small seed in a 24 mm cell. Nine hertz on mild
red oak is the preferred agitation of Equation (1). Three hertz on the 16 Hz row is the quiet side
of the near-tie: the sample mean broke the tie toward 3 Hz, and on the plant, with α = 0.55, that
command keeps feff near 9.3 Hz while 9 Hz would push it near 12.6 Hz. A second draw can select
9 Hz. The efficiency gain over a silent shaker remains either way. Bur oak still misses about 31
percent of spacings. The cell did not grow. The claim is a recovery from 12% efficiency to 44%,
not a maize-grade meter [6, 12].

Conclusion

The coordinator is a one-step constrained search over shaker frequency and assist vacuum, with
depth removed from the search by a servo. It is predictive control in the receding-horizon sense
and a lookup in implementation. Withholding the bridge index is part of the design: the factor
regime has to be good when the feed loop is only tracking. The estimate says that regime is
low-frequency agitation matched to seed size, no assist vacuum on this cell, and a refusal to add
shaker frequency on top of an already harsh frame. A bench that reversed the sign of frequency
would refute the shape of Equation (1) and would not, by itself, refute the grid, the slew, or the
separation from the feed loop. Those are the parts that remain if the coefficients are replaced by
measurements.
