A Vibration--Wavelet--Coefficient Feature
for Rotating-Machinery Faults
Short paper on the first innovation of the expanded study
based on You, Fan, Li, Liang, Fang, and Wang,
Shock and Vibration, 2019, Article ID 1908485
Abstract
Fault description for a vertical axial-flow pump has to keep three facts that a single transform
discards. Vibration severity measures how hard the casing is moving, and on this rig it separates
an eccentric axle from healthy running while leaving a bearing-pedestal fault almost unchanged.
A dyadic wavelet energy time-spectrum shows which scale carries the record, so a short transient is
not averaged away by one Fourier transform of the whole capture. The coefficient power spectrum
of that loudest scale then reads seven shaft orders, from half order through the sixth harmonic,
on the radial, tangential, and axial channels. One severity scalar and twenty-one spectral samples
form the vibration--wavelet--coefficient (VWC) vector in R22. This short paper specifies that map,
shows why the three blocks are not substitutes for one another, and records what the published
pump signatures look like inside it. The vector is the common input of every classifier in the
parent study. The search that labels it is a separate innovation.
1
Introduction
A rotating machine writes its condition into casing acceleration, but the raw record is the wrong
object to classify. At the duty used here the shaft turns at 960 r/min, so the fundamental is f0 =
n/60 = 16 Hz, and a capture of 8192 samples at 10 kHz lasts 0.8192 s, about 13.1 revolutions, with
a Fourier bin of 1.221 Hz. That grid resolves 3f0 = 48 Hz from the 50 Hz supply line. It does not by
itself say which number a maintenance decision needs.
Three descriptions compete. Root-mean-square velocity, the vibration severity, is the standardized
intensity [5]. On the published radial records it is 1.051 mm/s when the pump is healthy, 3.737 mm/s
with an eccentric axle, 1.161 mm/s with a bearing-pedestal fault, and 1.700 mm/s with sealing-ring
wear [1]. The eccentric state is obvious on this axis. The pedestal state is a ten-percent change and
cannot be thresholded safely. A single Fourier spectrum of the whole capture sees shaft orders and
also averages any impact that does not last for the 0.82 s record [4]. A wavelet energy vector sees the
loud scale and does not say whether the loudness sits at 8 Hz or at 16 Hz [2]. VWC keeps all three,
and nothing else, so the interface to a classifier stays a vector of dimension 22.

Dyadic energy
A mother wavelet ψ of zero mean and unit norm generates the dyadic family ψ2j,b(t) = 2−j/2ψ(2−j(t−

g, then downsamples both branches by two. The high-pass branch at level j is cDj. The energy

Training and test rows must use the same coordinate order. Before classification, each coordinate
is scaled to the unit interval by a range fit on the training rows only and frozen for the test rows.
Severity is near 1 mm/s and a coefficient power sample is near 10−3; without this map the radial
kernel would ignore the spectral block.
The published reading of the pump records is then a statement about x [1]. Healthy running
pairs a moderate severity with a tangential spectrum peaked at 16 Hz. The published tangential
peak power there is 1.683 × 10−3. Eccentric axle raises severity by the factor 3.56 and raises that
tangential peak to 3.322 × 10−3, which is the stationary form of a larger unbalance force. Bearing-
pedestal fault leaves severity almost alone and moves the tangential peak to 8 Hz, at a published
power 4.701 × 10−3, about 2.8 times the healthy tangential peak. Sealing-ring wear sets severity at
1.70 mm/s and emphasizes f0, half order, and 2f0, with the tangential channel again the largest. The
pedestal ratio is the empirical reason the vector contains both blocks: the spectral coordinate moves
by a larger factor than the severity coordinate. Figure 2 is an illustrative baseband picture of that
emphasis, not a laboratory spectrum.

What this innovation does and does not do
VWC is deterministic once the wavelet, the depth, the integrator, and the tie break are fixed. This
specification uses the Daubechies wavelet with four vanishing moments and eight levels, which the
2019 paper does not name; the name is part of the exposition, and the definition of k⋆does not
depend on it. The map costs one linear filter bank per axis plus one Fourier transform of the winning
coefficient sequence. At N = 8192 that cost is negligible beside any later parameter search. Two
hundred and forty vectors, sixty per state, are the entire labeled sample the vector is asked to support
[1].
The innovation does not label the vector. Healthy, eccentric, pedestal, and seal-wear patterns still
overlap, and the pedestal pattern is the least stable because resonance and site interference occupy

5
Conclusion
The first innovation replaces an unstructured vibration record with a 22-dimensional description:
radial vibration severity, the identity of the loudest dyadic scale, and the coefficient power of seven
shaft orders on three casing axes. The blocks answer different questions, and the published severities
show that the intensity question and the order question disagree on the pedestal fault. The map
