sample counts, and classification rates in Chapter 3 are the values reported by You, Fan,
Li, Liang, Fang, and Wang (2019). One cell of their printed training-count table is arith-

Time-domain traces, baseband spectra, and dyadic energy bars are illustrative signals

are not the laboratory recordings, and they are not offered as a new experiment. Block

Abstract
Fault diagnosis of rotating machinery separates into two coupled tasks: extracting a de-
scription of the vibration that still carries the fault, and assigning that description to
an operating state. This paper develops both tasks for a vertical axial-flow pump that
is observed in four states: normal running, eccentric-axle fault, bearing-pedestal fault,
and sealing-ring wear. The feature stage, called VWC, joins three descriptions of the
same record. Vibration severity compresses the record to a single intensity. A dyadic
wavelet energy time-spectrum shows how that intensity is distributed across scales. The
coefficient power spectrum of the maximum-energy scale then reads the shaft harmonics
1
2f0, f0, 2f0, . . . , 6f0 on which the mechanical faults write their signatures. Three orthog-
onal directions and one severity scalar produce a vector of dimension 22. The decision
stage is a radial-basis support vector machine whose penalty and kernel width are chosen
by a modified shuffled frog-leaping algorithm. The modification replaces the usual two-
step leap of the worst frog by a distance-weighted leap that learns from both the local
and the global leader, and it replaces a failed random restart by a Gaussian perturba-
tion of the current frog. On the published pump data---180 training vectors and 60 test
vectors---the resulting classifier reaches a pooled test accuracy of 93.3%, above the back-
propagation network (85.0%), the chemically inspired SVM (91.7%), and the unmodified
frog-leaping SVM (91.7%). The bearing-pedestal state remains the hardest of the four,
which is consistent with resonance and site interference in that record. The paper sets out
the mechanical and statistical principles, the prior art those principles have produced, the
precise problem left open, the two algorithms in full, the construction of the acquisition
system, and a quantitative reading of the published estimate.

Chapter 1
Research of Previous Work
Diagnosis of rotating machinery sits at the meeting point of rotor dynamics, transducer
physics, nonstationary signal analysis, and statistical decision theory. A pump, a turbine,
a gearbox, or a motor produces a vibration record whether it is healthy or not. The record
is long, the fault is often weak, and the label that an engineer needs is short: which of a
few known states is present, and how urgently it should be acted on. This chapter sets
down the principles that make that reduction possible, reviews the technical lines that
have already been tried, and then states the specific gap that the VWC front end and the
MSFLA-SVM classifier are built to close. The exposition follows the machine from the
forces in the rotor to the features in a classifier, because the later chapters reuse the same
objects.
1.1
Principles and technology of vibration-based diag-
nosis
1.1.1
The rotor as a forced dynamical system
A rotating machine is a dynamical system whose state can be idealized, at the first level
of description, as the motion of a shaft in a set of bearings, driven by a torque and loaded
by a fluid or a process force. Let the lateral displacement of a rotor station be the vector
q(t) ∈R2, with components along two directions fixed to the casing. A linear model that
is already rich enough to explain the spectral signatures used later is
M¨q(t) + C ˙q(t) + Kq(t) = fu(t) + fb(t) + ff(t) + fe(t).
(1.1)
Here M, C, and K are mass, damping, and stiffness operators. They include the shaft,
the impeller or the disks, the bearing films or the rolling elements, and the pedestal. The
four forces on the right-hand side are the usual sources of diagnostically useful vibration.
The unbalance force fu is produced when the mass center of a rotating part does not

The last term, fe, collects everything the laboratory does not control: neighboring
machines, the 50 Hz electromagnetic field of the supply, and sensor cable motion. In the
pump records a line at 50 Hz is reported in the healthy spectrum and is identified as
supply interference. Any feature set that cannot tell 3f0 = 48 Hz from 50 Hz will confuse
a mechanical harmonic with an electrical one. The frequency resolution of the acquisition,
discussed in Chapter 3, has to be finer than that 2 Hz gap.
Equation (1.1) is linear and time-invariant only as a teaching model. Real faults make
C and K depend on q and on time: a loose pedestal, a breathing crack, a rub, or a
worn seal all modulate the operators. The vibration then fails to be a stationary sinusoid,
which is why a pure Fourier catalogue is not a sufficient front end. The principles below
exist to recover a stable description anyway.
1.1.2
From force to a measured voltage
The quantity in (1.1) is a displacement of the shaft. The instrument in the pump ex-
periment is a triaxial charge accelerometer on the casing, not a pair of proximity probes
looking at the shaft. Casing acceleration is the second derivative of a filtered version of
q, filtered by the transfer function of the bearing, the pedestal, and the casing. If Hij(ω)
is that transfer function from force direction j to measured direction i, the acceleration
spectrum is
Ai(ω) = −ω2 X

Two consequences follow at once. High-frequency resonances are amplified by the −ω2
factor and by peaks of Hij, so a small impact can dominate the acceleration record while
contributing little displacement. Low-frequency unbalance, which is obvious in a displace-
ment orbit, can look modest in acceleration until it is integrated back toward velocity.
Vibration severity, defined on velocity rather than on acceleration, exists for exactly this
reason: standards judge the destructive potential of a motion by the velocity, whose
energy sits closer to the frequency band that actually fatigues the machine.
A charge accelerometer converts acceleration into charge. The B&K 4321 used on the
pump has a nominal sensitivity of 9.8 pC/g, a mounted resonance high enough that the
stated usable band runs from 0.1 Hz to 12 kHz, and a range of 500 g. Charge is converted
to voltage by a charge amplifier, and that voltage is what the acquisition card samples.

Vapnik's statistical learning theory [15, 16, 12] answers the small-sample fact with a
capacity control. The probability of a large gap between training error and test error
is bounded by a term that grows with the VC-dimension of the hypothesis class and
shrinks with n. A hyperplane in a feature space of huge dimension would have a huge
VC-dimension if it were unconstrained.
The support vector machine constrains it by
maximizing the margin, and the kernel trick realizes that hyperplane in a space the user
never writes down. The practical price is two continuous parameters: the soft-margin
penalty, which decides how many training points may fall inside the margin, and the
width of the radial basis, which decides how finely the kernel measures distance. Set
either of them badly and the capacity-control argument no longer describes the machine
that was actually trained.
Parameter search is therefore part of the classifier, not a
preliminary inconvenience. Grid search, genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization,
artificial bee colony search, chemical-reaction search, and shuffled frog-leaping search are
all attempts to set those two numbers from the training data alone. Section 1.2 compares
them by the way they move through the parameter space. Section 1.3 states why the
unmodified frog-leaping search is not yet a satisfactory mover, which is the reason for the
modification developed in Chapter 2.
1.1.7
What the technology chain must guarantee
The principles above impose five obligations on any system that claims to diagnose this
pump.
1. The sensor and the clock must be fast enough for the band they claim to see, and
slow enough that the record still contains many shaft revolutions. At 10 kHz and
8192 samples the record holds about 13.1 revolutions and resolves the mains line
from 3f0. That pair of facts is the acquisition specification.
2. The feature map must retain intensity, because severity is the standardized measure
of damage, and it must retain the distribution of energy across F in three directions,
because severity does not separate the classes.
15


===== PAGE 19 =====
3. The feature map must be able to notice a weak transient that a single full-length
Fourier transform would average away. A dyadic energy spectrum is one construction
that does so without giving up an exact notion of scale.
4. The classifier must be stable at n = 180, d = 22, four classes. Margin methods meet
that requirement more naturally than unrestricted multilayer networks.
5. The parameters of the classifier must be chosen by a search that does not collapse
onto the first strong training vector it finds. On a 60-vector test set, a five-point
swing in accuracy is three samples. A search that overfits the training fold will
spend those three samples.
The rest of the chapter asks how far the published literature has already carried these
five obligations, and where it stops short of the pump problem as posed by You and
co-authors.
1.2
Previous work on features, classifiers, and their
combination
The literature is large enough that a flat list of papers would hide the structure. The
review is organized by the technical idea each line contributed, and each idea is tied back
to one of the five obligations above. Citations concentrate on rotating machinery, with the
neighboring lines (inverters, process plants, heat pumps) included only where the same
classifier or the same fusion argument was developed and later imported.
1.2.1
Symptom catalogues and analytic redundancy
The oldest diagnostic tradition is a catalogue of symptoms written by people who had
dismantled failed machines. Sohre's account of high-speed turbomachinery [2, 76] sorts
operating problems into a small number of mechanical families---unbalance, misalignment,
looseness, rub, bearing damage, resonance, and a handful of hydraulic and aerodynamic
mechanisms---and lists the spectral clues an analyst should expect from each. The nine-
family, thirty-seven-type partition cited in the source paper is this kind of catalogue.
It remains the right vocabulary for the four pump states. Eccentric axle is unbalance.
Bearing pedestal is a looseness that begins in a rolling-element fault. Sealing-ring wear
is a hydraulic leakage that loads the rotor. The catalogue tells the analyst which lines
in F ought to move. It does not compute the lines, and it does not assign a new record
automatically.
A second early tradition replaces the catalogue by a model. Beard's thesis [3] showed
that a linear system can be fitted with analytic redundancy: a bank of observers, each
16


===== PAGE 20 =====
of which assumes a different fault, produces a residual that is small only under its own
assumption. Willsky's survey and Frank's survey [4, 13] established residual generation
as a discipline, with detection as a decision on the residual and isolation as a decision
on which residual moved. Sliding-mode observers, as developed by Edwards, Spurgeon,
and Patton [22], keep the residual well behaved when the plant is uncertain, by forcing
the estimation error onto a surface in finite time. Persis and Isidori [24] gave geometric
conditions under which a nonlinear system admits a filter that is sensitive to one fault
and invariant to the others. Isermann's review [33] then placed model-based detection
next to the industrial systems that can actually support it: the model has to be accurate
in the frequency band of the fault, the disturbances have to be characterizable, and the
sensors have to observe the states the residual needs.
For a vertical axial-flow pump in a closed water loop, that last requirement is the
obstacle. The forces in (1.1) depend on the seal gap, the bearing clearance, the dissolved
gas, and the precise pedestal stiffness, none of which is known to the accuracy a resid-
ual would need in order to be smaller than the fault. The experiment measures casing
acceleration and almost nothing else. A data-driven front end is not a rejection of the
model-based line. It is what remains when the residual cannot be built. The later numer-
ical comparison is therefore entirely among data-driven classifiers that share one feature
vector.

A second construction keeps the coefficients themselves, or a statistic of them, inside

keep the scale whose kurtosis or energy is extreme, and only then compute a spectrum.

wavelet at all. It measures, in each frequency bin, the normalized fourth moment of the
short-time Fourier amplitudes, and it peaks where the signal is impulsive. The coefficient
power spectrum of the maximum-energy wavelet level is a cousin of this idea. First find the
scale that holds the energy, then read the periodic structure inside that scale. The pump
method adds the severity scalar and the three-axis repetition, which spectral kurtosis by
itself does not supply.
A third construction uses complex or continuous wavelets when the phase, not only the
energy, carries the fault. Seshadrinath, Singh, and Panigrahi [56] apply complex wavelets

separated from the vibration signature when the transform preserves phase relations that
a real orthogonal wavelet discards. Khalil and Bayoumi [77] had earlier used a dyadic
wavelet affine invariant for a different recognition task; the citation matters here because
it records that the dyadic grid is stable under the shifts a real acquisition produces. The
pump records are triaxial acceleration at a fixed speed, not a variable-frequency electrical
drive, so the simpler real dyadic energy is the appropriate member of the family. The
complex-wavelet result remains the warning that a phase-blind energy can fail when the
fault is a change of timing rather than a change of intensity.
Bin, Gao, Li, and Dhillon [53] combine wavelet packets with empirical mode decom-
position for early rotor faults, then classify ten rotor states with a neural network. The
combination is a useful reference point because it admits, by existing, that neither the
packet energies nor the modes are always sufficient alone. VWC is a different combination:
a time-domain standard (severity), a scale distribution (the energy time-spectrum), and a
harmonic reading of the loudest scale (the coefficient power spectrum). It is deliberately
small. Twenty-two numbers are the entire interface between the signal and the classifier.
1.2.3
The empirical-mode line
Lei, Lin, He, and Zuo [54] review empirical mode decomposition in rotating-machine
diagnosis and list the faults for which a mode energy, a mode entropy, or a Hilbert
envelope spectrum has been used as a feature. Dybaªa and Zimroz [57] show that the
early stage of a rolling-bearing fault, still buried in the raw spectrum, becomes visible

and Huang's ensemble empirical mode decomposition [42] attacks the mode-mixing that
made two records of the same fault produce differently ordered modes: each sift is run on
the signal plus a different noise, and the modes are averaged. Zhang, Liang, Zhou, and
Zang [63] put permutation entropy of the ensemble modes beside an optimized SVM for
bearings. Zhao, Wang, Ma, and Li [67] use complete ensemble decomposition, a short-

industrial cousin of the axial-flow pump in this paper. Yang, Liu, Li, Li, and Ma [65, 40]
extract gear features by the ensemble decomposition and classify them by an SVM whose
parameters are set by an artificial bee colony. Huang, Hu, and Yang [47] do the same
service for a high-voltage circuit breaker with the plain decomposition, an energy entropy,
and a genetic algorithm around the SVM.
Two lessons travel from that line into the present design, and one does not.
The
first lesson is that a demodulated or scale-local spectrum sees an early fault that the raw
Fourier transform misses. The coefficient power spectrum is the wavelet version of that
lesson. The second lesson is that the SVM, not the particular adaptive decomposition,
is what turns the feature into a label when the sample is small, and that the SVM's two
parameters are worth a global search. The lesson that does not travel is the adaptive
partition itself. On sixty records per class, an adaptive mode ordering is an extra source
of variance between the training fold and the test fold. A fixed dyadic filter bank removes
that variance. The price is a bank that is not matched to each record. The maximum-
energy rule gives back a limited adaptivity: the scale index k may differ from record to
record even though the filters do not.
Local mean decomposition, which Smith defined [34] for nonstationary physiological
signals and which Tian, Ma, Lu, and Wang later paired [62] with a singular-value step
and an extreme learning machine for bearings under variable conditions, belongs to the
same adaptive family. Variational mode decomposition, which Dragomiretskiy and Zosso
posed [59] as an optimization rather than a sift, has largely replaced ad-hoc sifting in
newer work because the modes are concurrent and the bandwidth of each mode is a
penalty term. Neither is used here. They are recorded so that the choice of a dyadic bank
is visible as a choice.

1.2.5
Support vector machines and the search for their parame-
ters
Cortes and Vapnik's soft-margin classifier [16], and the earlier hard-margin algorithm
of Boser, Guyon, and Vapnik [14], are the decision rule used for the pump once the
features exist. Widodo and Yang's review [39, 41] collects the first decade of that rule in
condition monitoring: bearing faults, gear faults, induction machines, and pumps, with
linear, polynomial, and radial kernels, and with the repeated observation that the radial
kernel is the one practitioners keep. The geometry and the dual problem are derived in
Section 2.2. The prior art that matters here is the outer loop that chooses the penalty C

and the kernel width.
A grid search evaluates C and the width on a logarithmic lattice and keeps the pair
with the best cross-validated accuracy. It is simple, and it is expensive in the number
of trainings. It also cannot spend evaluations where the surface is interesting, because
the lattice is chosen in advance. Bordoloi and Tiwari [58, 17] compare a grid, a genetic
algorithm, and an artificial bee colony on a four-class gear problem, which is the same
number of classes as the pump, and find that the evolutionary searches reach accurate
multi-fault partitions. Holland's genetic algorithm maintains a population of candidate
pairs, selects the accurate ones, and recombines them. Its operators do not use the slope
of the accuracy surface, which is an advantage because cross-validated accuracy is a step
function of C and of the width.
Kennedy and Eberhart's particle swarm [18] moves
each candidate toward its own best position and toward the swarm's best position, with
a momentum term.
Dong and Luo [55, 20] use that swarm to set the parameters of
a least-squares SVM, after a principal-component compression, for bearing degradation
prediction. Suykens and Vandewalle's least-squares variant replaces the inequality con-
straints of the standard SVM by equalities, so the training step is a linear system. It is
faster and it loses the sparse set of support vectors. The pump classifier stays with the
standard soft-margin dual.
Karaboga's artificial bee colony searches by employed bees, onlookers, and scouts,
and it is the optimizer inside Yang's gear classifier. Alatas's artificial chemical reaction
optimization [49] treats candidate solutions as molecules and moves them by synthesis,
decomposition, and collision reactions. Ao, Cheng, Li, and Truong [61] wrap that reaction
search around an SVM for roller bearings, with a local characteristic-scale energy entropy
as the feature. Their ACROA-SVM is the second baseline of the pump study. It is a
strong baseline: on the published test set it ties the unmodified frog-leaping SVM at
91.7% pooled accuracy.
The shuffled frog-leaping algorithm of Eusuff and Lansey, later elaborated with Pasha [29,
37], was built for discrete water-network design and then used as a general memetic search.
A population of frogs is partitioned into memeplexes. Inside a memeplex the worst frog
leaps toward the best frog of that memeplex, and if the leap fails, toward the best frog of
the whole population. If that also fails, the worst frog is replaced at random. Memes, in
the sense of the memetic algorithm, are the local improvements; the shuffle that follows
the local search is the global mixing, inherited in spirit from the particle swarm. The
appeal, as the source paper states it, is a short parameter list, a simple leap, and a search
that is partly local and partly global. The defect, also stated there, appears as the iter-
ation count grows. Frogs converge, the memeplexes stop differing from one another, and
the random replacement of a failed frog is a blind jump that throws away the position
already reached. The modified leap in Chapter 2 is aimed at those two defects and at
nothing else. It does not change the SVM. It changes the way C and the kernel parameter
21


===== PAGE 25 =====
are proposed.
A practical guide belongs in this review because the search is only as good as the
training procedure inside it. Chang and Lin's LIBSVM [48, 19] is the standard solver
for the dual, using Platt's sequential minimal optimization on pairs of multipliers. Hsu,
Chang, and Lin's guide [52] fixes the conventions that the search must respect: scale
each feature to a common range before training, search C and the radial width on a
log grid first so that the evolutionary search has a sane box, and judge candidates by
cross-validation rather than by training accuracy. The pump pipeline normalizes before
classification, which is the first of those conventions. The fitness of a frog is a cross-
validated classification rate, which is the third. The source study does not publish the
box over which C and the width were searched. The exposition in Chapter 2 therefore
states the box as an algorithmic object and does not pretend to recover a number the
experiment never printed.
Schölkopf and Smola's kernel monograph [51, 11] supplies the positive-definiteness
condition, going back to Mercer, that lets the radial basis be a kernel at all. Without
that condition the dual quadratic program need not be convex and the solver's output
need not be a margin maximizer. The radial basis used here satisfies the condition for
every positive width, so the only remaining question is which width, together with which
penalty, the leap should return.
1.2.6
Fusion, Bayesian networks, and neighboring plants
Not every fault is carried by one vibration sensor.
Cai, Huang, and Xie [68] review
Bayesian networks as a way to fuse uncertain symptoms: each node is a fault or a test,
and the edges are conditional probabilities that a test is positive given a fault. Cai, Liu,
Fan, and co-authors [60] apply that construction to a ground-source heat pump, fusing
several information sources, and report a clear gain over a single-source decision. Cai,
Zhao, Liu, and Xie [69] later give a data-driven Bayesian treatment of inverter faults in a
permanent-magnet drive. Yin, Zhu, and Kaynak [66] keep a partial least-squares model
and focus the detection statistic on the key performance indicators of the plant rather
than on every measured channel equally. Venkatasubramanian's three-part review [28] of
process fault detection places these ideas in a larger map: quantitative model residuals,
qualitative symptom reasoning, and historical data methods.
The axial-flow pump experiment is a historical-data method with a single triaxial
sensor. It does not estimate a Bayesian network. The fusion it does perform is early
fusion, inside the feature vector: three directions, seven harmonics, one severity. The heat-
pump result is relevant as a ceiling. When independent symptoms exist, a network that
multiplies their likelihoods can beat a classifier that sees only vibration. The published
pump comparison does not have those extra symptoms, so the ceiling is not available, and
22


===== PAGE 26 =====
the gains reported in Chapter 3 are gains of feature and of parameter search only.
A number of SVM papers in the source bibliography sit outside rotating machinery:
open-circuit detection in drives, intrusion detection, vehicle suspension, biochemical pro-
cesses. They establish that the same (C, width) problem appears whenever an SVM is
the decision rule. They are not evidence about pumps. This review uses them only to
support the narrow claim that parameter search is part of the method.
1.2.7
What the previous work has already settled
The lines above agree on more than they dispute.
*  Spectral symptoms organized by shaft order are the right language for unbalance,
looseness, and hydraulic once-per-revolution faults. Sohre's catalogue and the bear-
ing kinematics (1.3) are still the interpretation layer sitting on top of any classifier.
*  A fixed-window spectrum is a weak detector of early impulsive faults.
Wavelet
energies, wavelet-packet energies, spectral kurtosis, and empirical-mode envelope
spectra are the repairs, and they work for the same reason.
*  On a few hundred labeled records, a margin classifier with a radial kernel is a
stronger default than an unrestricted neural network. The published pump tables
reproduce this general experience.
*  The radial SVM does not choose its own penalty and width. Grid search, genetic
algorithms, particle swarms, bee colonies, chemical-reaction search, and frog-leaping
search are all credible outer loops, and on gear and bearing benchmarks the evolu-
tionary loops beat a coarse grid.
*  Fusion of independent sensors helps when the sensors exist. A triaxial accelerometer
is a minimal fusion, and it is the one the pump rig provides.
1.3
The problem this paper addresses
1.3.1
The gap between the settled results and the pump
The settled results do not by themselves diagnose the vertical axial-flow pump. Four
concrete gaps remain, and they are the problem statement.
The first gap is the feature gap. Severity is standardized and, on this rig, almost blind
to the pedestal fault: 1.161 mm/s against 1.051 mm/s in the healthy state. A Fourier
spectrum sees the healthy blade-pass line at 64 Hz and the supply line at 50 Hz, and it
averages any transient that does not last for the whole 0.82 s record. A wavelet energy

The second gap is the stationarity gap, stated in the source introduction and repeated
here as an obligation. The vibration of the pump under an early fault is a strong periodic
carrier plus a weak, nonstationary disturbance plus interference. Methods whose only
transform is (1.16) are, on the source authors' own account, inaccurate for that mixture.
The method has to analyze the record in time and in scale, and it still has to return the
harmonic amplitudes a mechanical interpretation requires. The energy time-spectrum
followed by a coefficient power spectrum is a specific answer to that double requirement.
Chapter 2 writes the answer in equations and fixes the implementation choices the source
text leaves implicit.
The third gap is the search gap. The shuffled frog-leaping algorithm already combines
a local leap and a global leap, which is why it is a reasonable optimizer for (C, g). As
the source formulates the defect: once frogs begin to agree, diversity collapses, the search
sits in a local optimum of the cross-validated accuracy, and a failed frog is replaced by
a uniform random draw that does not use the position it is abandoning. On a fitness
surface that is a step function of two parameters, a blind draw is not a rare event, and
each draw costs a full cross-validated training. The modification has to do two things and
stop. It has to let the worst frog learn from the local leader and the global leader in the
same leap, with weights that depend on how far the frog is from the global leader, so that
a frog already near the leader exploits and a frog far from the leader explores. It has to
replace the blind draw by a Gaussian perturbation of the current position, so that a failed
leap becomes a local mutation rather than a restart. Both changes are internal to the
leap. The fitness function remains the cross-validated SVM accuracy, and the decision
function remains the radial-basis SVM. MSFLA is that modified leap.
The fourth gap is the evaluation gap. A method that is only a derivation is not yet a
diagnosis. The pump rig supplies four states that a reader can understand mechanically,
a sample that is honestly small, and three published baselines: the backpropagation
network, the chemical-reaction SVM, and the unmodified frog-leaping SVM. The problem
includes reading that comparison at the resolution the tables support. Overall accuracy
hides the pedestal state, which is the worst class for every method. Training accuracy
hides the generalization drop, which is largest for the neural baseline. A single pooled
percentage hides the fact that the entire test set is sixty vectors, so a difference of one
vector is 1.7 percentage points. Chapter 3 performs that reading. It does not invent a

1.3.2
Objectives
The paper has four objectives, matched to the four gaps.
1. Derive and specify the VWC map from a triaxial acceleration record to a vector in
R22: the severity, the dyadic energy time-spectrum, the selection of the maximum-
energy level, and the coefficient power spectrum at the seven shaft orders on three
axes.
2. Derive the soft-margin SVM with a radial kernel, then specify the modified frog-
leaping search that sets its penalty and its kernel parameter, including the distance-
weighted leap and the Gaussian mutation, and state which quantities the published
experiment does not report.
3. Describe the acquisition system as a build: the closed water loop, the four mechani-
cal states, the triaxial charge sensor, the sampling clock, the record length, and the

4. Estimate performance from the published counts. Report per-class and pooled rates,
the train-to-test drop of each method, and the special difficulty of the bearing-
pedestal state, with the arithmetic of the tables made explicit.
1.3.3
What is deliberately outside the problem
A precise problem also says what it will not claim. This paper does not claim a new
laboratory campaign. The severities, the hardware table, and the classification counts
come from You, Fan, Li, Liang, Fang, and Wang. Illustrative signals in the figures are
synthetic records scaled to those severities and shaped to the harmonic patterns the
source describes; each such figure is captioned as illustrative. The paper does not claim
that MSFLA is a universal optimizer, or that VWC is the unique admissible front end.
Packet energies, spectral kurtosis, and ensemble modes remain legitimate alternatives
where the sample and the hardware differ. The paper does not claim a detection delay,
a remaining-useful-life estimate, or a performance under variable speed. The rig runs at
one stated speed, 960 r/min. The paper does not claim an optimal C or an optimal kernel
width, because the source does not print them. It claims that the search procedure is
fully specified and that the published accuracy table is the estimate of its worth on this

Relation to the other outer loops
The four classifiers in the published comparison share the VWC vector and differ in the
map from that vector to a label.
The backpropagation network fits (1.25) by gradi-

collisions, accepted by a reaction criterion. SFLA-SVM proposes (C, g) by the cascade
(2.36) and by random replacement. MSFLA-SVM proposes (C, g) by (2.41) and (2.42).
Any difference in the tables of Chapter 3 is therefore a difference of inductive bias and of
search, on a common feature. It is not a difference of sensors or of labels.
A useful way to see the modified leap is to write the expected step, conditional on the
current frogs, ignoring the clip. Because each rand(0, 1) has mean one half,

it moves toward Fb alone with probability one on the first try, and toward Fg only on
failure. Equation (2.43) is biased toward exploration exactly when qi is large, which is
when FFdist(i, S) is large. That is the diversity mechanism, expressed as a conditional
expectation rather than as a slogan. The Gaussian mutant adds a zero-mean perturbation
on the failure event, so it does not change (2.43) on the event that the leap succeeds, and
it prevents the failure event from sampling the box uniformly.
2.2.11
Numerical safeguards
A few safeguards keep the procedure inside the theory that justifies it. The kernel matrix
is positive definite up to rounding; a practical solver adds a ridge of size about 10−8
to the diagonal, which is a negligible shift of C−1 inside the box (2.32). Features enter
only after (2.18), so a change of engineering unit cannot masquerade as a change of g.
Fitness is computed on stratified folds, so a frog cannot score perfectly by ignoring the
pedestal class, which has the same training count as the others but is harder. The test
set is touched once, after the return of Fg. Repeating the search from several random
populations and keeping the best training fitness would be a further outer loop and would
require a fresh test set to stay honest. The published estimate is the result of the search
the source ran, reported once.

2.2.16
Operation count and a reference schedule
The cost of one fitness evaluation dominates the cost of the feature map, and it is worth
writing the count so that a schedule can be chosen without discovering it in a profiler. One
binary SVM on nb training rows, solved by sequential minimal optimization, is observed
in practice to scale between nb and roughly n2
b kernel evaluations, depending on how many
multipliers remain free. This exposition does not rely on a worst-case quadratic bound.
It counts trainings. A one-versus-one reduction on four classes trains six binary machines.
A stratified five-fold fitness trains each of them five times, on four fifths of the 180 rows.
One frog evaluation is therefore
Ntrain = 6 × 5 = 30
(2.66)
binary trainings, each on 144 rows, plus six cheap predictions on the complementary 36
rows. A local step that proposes one leap evaluates fitness once, and evaluates it a second

--- the procedure in Section 2.2 accepts the mutant whether or not it improves --- so an
implementation may skip the second evaluation and pay it only when the mutant becomes
the worst frog of a later step. Counting one evaluation per local step is the lower figure.
A reference schedule, offered as a software starting point and not as the unpublished
experimental setting, is M = 5 memeplexes, P = 10 frogs, L = 10 local steps per
memeplex per shuffle, and S = 30 shuffles. The number of fitness evaluations is then

(2.67)

in minutes. It is not a reason to reduce the fold count. Five folds on 180 stratified rows

coarser estimate of the same accuracy the frog is trying to maximize. The fitness surface
is already a step function; coarsening it further hides the one-vector improvements the
modification is designed to find.
The feature map, by comparison, is paid once per record. Eight steps of a length-8
filter bank on N = 8192 samples, three axes, plus one real transform of 256 coefficients,
is well under 106 arithmetic operations per record. Two hundred and forty records are
irrelevant next to NF. Precomputing Φ(a) and storing the 240 × 22 matrix, then letting
every frog see only that matrix, is the right order of work. Recomputing the wavelet bank
inside the fitness would multiply the map cost by NF and would not change a single label.
Memory is equally one-sided. The kernel matrix of one binary problem is 144 × 144
doubles, about 160 kB, and six such problems can be built and discarded in series. The
population stores 50 pairs of scalars. Nothing in the search requires a kernel matrix of the
full multiclass sample at once, because the six binary problems do not share multipliers.
They share only the pair (C, g).
2.2.17
What a correct implementation is obliged to log
Two implementations of the procedure can return different pairs (C, g) and the same test
labels, or the same pair and different labels if a tie in the vote is broken differently. A log
that makes the run auditable, given the raw records, contains six items.
1. The mother wavelet, the analysis depth J, and the tie break for k⋆.
2. The integrator that produced velocity, including the high-pass cutoff if one was used,
and which axis supplied the single severity coordinate.
3. The training ranges (u(j), v(j)) of (2.18), stored and reused on the test rows.
4. The box, Dmax, the population schedule (M, P, L, S), and the random seed of the
initial population.
5. The returned (log10 C, log10 g) and the five fold accuracies that make up F at that
pair.
6. The vote matrix on the sixty test rows: six binary decisions and the final label, so
that a tie and its break can be replayed.
The source paper prints none of these six, because it prints the classification counts
instead. This exposition prints the definitions that make the six meaningful, and it leaves
the numerical log to a run that has the records. The estimate in Chapter 3 does not need
the log. It needs the counts, and it has them. The log is the condition under which a
later run is allowed to say that it reproduced the method rather than only the tables.

2.2.18
Limits of the two technologies, stated as engineering con-
straints
VWC assumes a single dominant shaft speed known to an accuracy better than the half-
order window. At 960 r/min the half-order spacing is 8 Hz, and the sampler's window of
half an order is 4 Hz. A speed error of one percent is 0.16 Hz at f0 and 0.96 Hz at 6f0, still
inside the window. A speed error of ten percent walks 6f0 by 9.6 Hz and puts the sample
on the neighboring order. The rig statement of a fixed 960 r/min is therefore part of the
feature definition, not only part of the operating-point table. A variable-speed campaign
would have to track f0 per record, resample the orders from that estimate, and re-derive
the coefficient Nyquist check. None of that tracking is present here, and the published
accuracies do not transfer to it by analogy.
MSFLA assumes that the fitness F is a function of two continuous parameters and
that spending thirty binary trainings to learn its value at one point is acceptable. If the
decision rule grew a third parameter --- a selected subset of the twenty-two coordinates, or
a per-axis kernel width --- the same schedule would search a volume instead of a rectangle,
and 1550 evaluations would become a thin net. The modification of the leap does not
change that scaling. It changes the direction of each evaluation. Feature selection inside
the frog, which several of the genetic-algorithm papers in Chapter 1 do perform, is a
different algorithm and would need its own estimate. This paper keeps every coordinate.
The pedestal difficulty discussed in Chapter 3 may well live in a few of them, but the
tables do not isolate which ones, and a search over subsets on 180 rows would be able to
over-fit the folds faster than a search over (C, g).
The two constraints together say where the method is entitled to be trusted. Fixed
speed, four named states, triaxial casing acceleration, a 22-vector with one severity and
twenty-one shaft-order amplitudes, a shared radial kernel, and a search whose only job
is the pair (C, g): that is the operating envelope of the chapters that follow. Inside the
envelope the published test counts are the estimate. Outside it the chapters supply a
specification, and the specification is not a result.

3.1.2
The four operating states and how they are produced
The experiment defines four mutually exclusive states. The healthy state is the pump at
the duty of Table 3.1 with no intentional defect. The other three states are produced by
a physical change, not by a numerical simulation.

Eccentric-axle fault is produced by moving a weight on the shaft so that the mass
center leaves the geometric axis. In the language of (1.1) the change is an increase of
the unbalance me. The expected stationary signature is a rise of the force at f0 and,
because severity integrates velocity rather than a single Fourier coefficient, a rise of VIB.
Both are observed: severity goes from 1.051 to 3.737 mm/s, and the tangential coefficient
spectrum peaks at f0 with a larger amplitude than in the healthy state. The source also
notes energy at 28, 34, and 38 Hz and attributes it to crosstalk from other devices in the
laboratory. Those tones are not members of F. A feature map that sampled only F is,
by design, partly protected from them, and a feature map that used the whole spectrum
as a long vector would have to learn to ignore them from forty-five training records. This
is one practical reason the 22-vector is short.
Bearing-pedestal fault begins in the rolling-element bearing and is observed at the
pedestal that supports it. The source describes the usual bearing sequence: fatigue, wear,
and adhesion, followed by a periodic jump of the pedestal, a change of the support stiffness,
and an impact. The expected spectral signature of a stiffness that is not constant inside
one revolution includes a half-order line. The published reading matches that expectation
in the tangential direction, where the coefficient spectrum peaks at 1
2f0 = 8 Hz. Severity

3.1.5
Sample size, split, and the unit of analysis
The source collects 60 groups of data in each of the four states, 240 groups in all. Each
group is one 22-vector. Forty-five groups per state are the training sample and fifteen are
the test sample, so the design matrices have shapes
Xtrain ∈R180×22,
Xtest ∈R60×22.
(3.3)
The split is stratified by construction: each state contributes the same count to each
matrix. The source does not say whether the fifteen test groups were drawn at random,
taken from the end of each run, or chosen by hand. A repetition should draw them at
random inside each state and should freeze the draw before any search over (C, g). Because
the search described in Section 2.2 uses cross-validation inside the training matrix only,
the sixty test rows are available for a single estimate.
Sixty test vectors is a small denominator. One misclassified test vector moves a per-
class rate by 1/15 ≈6.67 percentage points and moves the pooled rate by 1/60 ≈1.67
percentage points. The difference between MSFLA-SVM and SFLA-SVM on the test set
is one vector in the pooled count (56 against 55). That difference is real as a count and
it is small as evidence. Section 3.2 reports it as a count.
The source remarks that more records could not be collected under the conditions of
the experiment, and that the SVM is used precisely because the sample is small. The
system build should be read with that constraint in view. The acquisition is adequate for a
four-class comparison at one duty. It is not a durability test, and it is not a variable-speed
campaign.

3.2.8
Reading the result against the problem statement
Section 1.3 asked for four things: a 22-dimensional feature that carries intensity, scale,

contribution inside the pedestal errors, not as a number the tables support.
Aliasing is the contribution the budget cannot bound from the printed table. The
sensor responds to 12 kHz and the converter samples at 10 kHz, so energy between 5
and 12 kHz folds into 0 to 5 kHz unless an analog filter stops it. Folded energy lands
preferentially in the fine wavelet levels, and it can change which level wins k⋆. If the
winning level moves, the coefficient rate changes and the amplitudes (2.16) are read on
a different series. The source plots show d5 winning for the displayed records, which is
evidence that, on those records, the mid band carried the energy, and it is not evidence
that an anti-alias filter was present. A repetition of the build should state the filter cutoff
next to the 10 kHz clock. Until that cutoff is known, high-frequency folding remains an
open term in the budget and a candidate contributor to the two pedestal test errors that
every SVM method still makes.
Interference tones that the order sampler does not aim at --- the reported crosstalk at
28, 34, and 38 Hz under the eccentric fault, and the supply line at 50 Hz --- are rejected
by the sampler's narrow windows and are not rejected by the wavelet energy that chooses
the level. They can change k⋆without entering x as their own coordinates. That indirect
path is the reason a clean mechanical spectrum and a clean scale choice are both part
of signal conditioning, which is the improvement the source itself defers to future work.
The budget's conclusion is short. Calibration and quantization are too small to explain
the published confusions. Speed error at the one-percent level is tolerable for the order
windows. Unfiltered high-frequency energy and in-band interference are large enough, in
mechanism, to move the pedestal features, and they are exactly the mechanisms named
in the source discussion of that class.

replaces step 7 and does not replace steps 1--6. That separation is what makes Table 3.5
a comparison of classifiers on one feature.
The procedure also says what the operator does not do. The operator does not choose
C and g by hand after looking at the test labels. The operator does not drop the pedestal
records because they are harder. The operator does not lengthen the test set by moving
training rows across the split after the search has run. Each of those moves would raise a
number in Table 3.5 and would destroy the estimate. The published counts are informative
because, as far as the source text shows, none of those moves was made.

Read across the test columns, the modified search matches the better of the other two
searches in every state: it matches SFLA-SVM on the eccentric axle (one error), matches

The practical statement that closes the estimate is therefore a statement about effort.
On this rig, with this sample, the VWC vector plus a radial SVM already carries the
diagnosis from the neural baseline's nine test errors to five. The modified leap carries it
from five to four, by avoiding one seal-wear error that the unmodified leap makes and
one eccentric error that the chemical-reaction search makes.

the problem the published counts show is still open.
