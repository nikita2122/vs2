A Modified Shuffled Frog-Leaping Search
for an SVM Fault Classifier
Short paper on the second innovation of the expanded study
based on You, Fan, Li, Liang, Fang, and Wang,
Shock and Vibration, 2019, Article ID 1908485
Abstract
A radial-basis support vector machine separates the 22-dimensional vibration features of a four-
state pump only after two numbers are chosen: the soft-margin penalty C and the kernel param-
eter g. Cross-validated accuracy, the natural score of a candidate pair, is a step function, so the
outer search has to be derivative-free. Shuffled frog-leaping already mixes a local leap toward
the best frog in a memeplex with a later leap toward the best frog in the population, and it
replaces a failed frog by a uniform draw. That cascade wastes a training whenever both leaders
would have helped at once, and the uniform draw abandons a position the search has already
paid for. The modification makes one leap, with weights set by distance to the global best, and
replaces a failure by a Gaussian perturbation of the current frog in logarithmic coordinates. On
the published split, 180 training vectors and 60 test vectors, the modified search labels 56 test
vectors correctly. The unmodified frog-leaping search and a chemical-reaction search each label
55. A backpropagation network labels 51. The gain over the other global searches is one test
vector, and it is reported as a count.

The unmodified leap and its two defects
A frog is a candidate u. The population is sorted by fitness and dealt round-robin into memeplexes,
so each memeplex receives a high-fitness frog [4, 5]. Inside a memeplex the worst frog Fw first steps
toward the local best Fb,

Only if that step fails to raise fitness does the same formula replace Fb by the global best Fg. If that
also fails, Fw is replaced by a uniform draw in the box. The clip enforces the box and a maximum
step. Two defects follow from the formulae. The two leaders are never used in the same step, so
a frog that needs a mixture of them spends a full cross-validated training to discover that the first
leader was not enough. And a failed frog is thrown away. Late in the run, when frogs already agree,
both defects shrink the diversity the shuffle was meant to protect [1].

5
Conclusion
The second innovation leaves the support vector machine and its radial kernel unchanged and changes
the proposal of (C, g). Every leap learns from the local best and the global best with weights set by
distance, and every failed leap mutates the current frog instead of restarting. On the published pump
split the resulting classifier makes four test errors. The unmodified leap and the chemical-reaction
search make five each. The pedestal class remains at two test errors for every SVM method, which
locates the open problem in the feature and in the interference, not in a further tweak of the same
two parameters.
