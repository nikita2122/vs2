Research for Solving Breakdown Risk in School Bag Factory Scheduling by Using
Status-Synchronized Digital Twin Coordination
Computer Science Short Paper
Companion to the system-design thesis
October 2026
Abstract
Machine status in a school-bag factory is not a label beside the schedule. Cutter wear and needle heat change the
next cycle time, the energy, and the chance that the cycle is interrupted. This paper presents status-synchronized
digital twin coordination with chance constraints (SSDT-CC). A live health state scales processing time, a hazard
gives a failure probability, and an assignment whose probability exceeds a tolerance is not deleted: a sixteen-
minute preventive maintenance renews the machine and the cycle is recomputed. The dispatcher is called again
whenever a resource becomes free, so the twin reschedules by rewriting the state the score reads. On twenty
accelerated-wear order books the coupled system finishes in1122minutes with0.81expected failures, against
about2059minutes and39expected failures for earliest due date. A tolerance sweep shows that a tighter limit
buys fewer failures with more maintenance. The plant uses expected delay, not a noisy sensor.
1 Introduction
Condition-based maintenance and digital twins are usually described as monitoring systems. A remaining-useful-life
model estimates when a tool will cross a failure threshold [5, 6]. A twin mirrors the machine for an operator or for
a planner who still writes the sequence by hand [2, 3, 1]. Scheduling research has started to put degradation inside
flexible job shops [11], most often as a cost or as a constraint on a plan that is built once. End-to-end multitarget
policies [12] still act on operation–machine pairs and do not renew a worn tool.
A school-bag shift does not offer that separation. The automatic cutter begins the day already worn. Running
it makes it slower, hotter, and more likely to stop. A schedule that treats processing time as a constant from the
routing sheet is using a health factor of one, whatever number is displayed on a screen. Inserting maintenance on a
clock, independently of which orders are waiting, spends the tool change on a machine the book does not need and
withholds it from a machine the book is about to overload.
The contribution is a closed loop with one action. Health is the twin. The chance constraint is the veto. The
action, when the veto fires, is a short renewal of that same machine, after which the dispatcher scores the shop again.
The score that chooses among bundles is the resource-coupled graph policy of the companion thesis. This paper
isolates what the health loop adds: the maps from health to time, energy, and failure, the maintenance rewrite, and
the estimates obtained when that rewrite is allowed or forbidden.

5 Why the local loss and the daily gain coexist
Table 3 puts the three ways of cutting one navy shell on one line. Maintenance is the slowest immediate occupation
and uses more energy than the manual cutter. It is the only option that both uses the fast machine and leaves that
machine below the hazard threshold.
The day is a sequence of such rows that share a health state. Three unrepaired cuts on Cutter-A charge about
102minutes and finish near health0.68, where the next shell is both slower and more likely to fail. One renewal and
then three cuts charge about82minutes and stay near the renewed health. Twelve such renewals on a worn book
are192minutes of tool-change time. The repair delay they avoid, relative to a due-date rule that never renews, is
on the order of1800minutes. The local row and the daily table are not in conflict. They answer different questions:
whether this cycle, alone, is shorter with a knife change, and whether the shift is shorter if worn cycles are allowed
to compound.

6 What this estimate includes
The tables use the hazard (4), the renewal (5), and the deterministic wear recursion (2). They do not use a random
breakdown draw, so a second run with the same seeds reproduces the means. They do not use the Kalman correction.
A noisy current or temperature would be reduced to the unit interval and then filtered; until that residual exists,
filtering it would be theatre. They also do not train a deep network. The dispatcher that proposes the bundle is the
identified one-hop score of the companion paper, and the twin’s only extra authority is the maintenance bit on that
bundle.
An ordinary book, included so the wear table is not the only evidence, finishes under the coupled system in1052
minutes and168 kWhwith0.91expected failures and5.4renewals. First-in-first-out on the same twenty books
takes1521minutes and303 kWhwith18.0expected failures and no renewals. The graph score without renewal sits
between them, at1234minutes,211 kWh, and10.1expected failures. Health-aware dispatch and health renewal are
different levers. The second lever is the one this paper isolates, and it is the one that moves failures from the teens,
or from about thirty on a worn day, to under one.
7 Conclusion
SSDT-CC keeps machine health inside the dispatch loop of a school-bag shop. The health state scales time and
energy, a hazard supplies the failure probability, and a chance constraint inserts a sixteen-minute partial renewal
instead of deleting the worn machine. One cycle does not repay the renewal. A worn day does: about twelve renewals
replace on the order of thirty expected failures and several hundred minutes of makespan. The Kalman update is the
form to use when a sensor is noisy. The tables were produced with a deterministic wear recursion, so they estimate
the chosen risk and not a particular factory’s next breakdown.
